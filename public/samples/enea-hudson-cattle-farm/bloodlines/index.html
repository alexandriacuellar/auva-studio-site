01 Pedigree

The bloodlines

Highlands have been the heart of the farm since the 1920s. In 1987 the family added a small registered Angus herd, kept as a separate purebred line and used to breed Highland × Angus crosses. Both herds are closed, so every animal we offer traces back through lines the family has documented.

  1. Foundation sire lineRecorded since 1924

    The Glen Ard line

    Traced through eleven generations of herd bulls to the family’s first registered Highland bull. Long, deep-bodied cattle with an unhurried temperament.

  2. Sire lineIntroduced 1962

    The Kinloch line

    Brought in for hardiness. Heavy winter coats, sound feet, and daughters that calve unassisted well into their teens.

  3. Sire lineIntroduced 1998

    The Ben Rua line

    Our most recent outcross, chosen for conformation: level toplines, correct legs, and the dun and silver coats now seen through the herd.

  4. Angus lineIntroduced 1987

    The Blackwillow line

    A small registered Angus herd, chosen for calving ease, growth, and an easy-fleshing, moderate frame. Kept purebred, and the source of the Angus side of every cross.

  5. CrossbredBred since 1990

    The Highland × Angus cross

    Highland hardiness and foraging with Angus growth and marbling. Hybrid vigor shows in thrift on grass, fertility, and easy calving. Most are black or dun with a shorter shaggy coat, and most are polled, since the Angus hornless gene is dominant.

  • Two closed purebred herds: no outside females, one carefully chosen bull every few years
  • Purebreds registered with the American Highland Cattle Association or the American Angus Association, with full pedigree papers
  • Crosses sold with both parents’ pedigrees and the dam’s calving record
  • Pedigrees recorded in the family herd book back to the first generation
  • Selected for temperament, hardiness, and conformation, in that order

Since 1924

The herd book

Every calf born on the farm since 1924 is in the family herd book: its sire and dam, the day it was born, and a line or two on how it grew. The early pages are in our great-grandfather’s hand. The newest are printed, but we still write the calving notes in ink.

When you buy from us, we copy the animal’s pages for you, back as many generations as you would like.

Husbandry

How the herd is kept

Highlands are among the most self-reliant cattle in the world, and Angus and the crosses do just as well on grass. Our part is to keep them on good grass and handle them often.

  1. I

    Rotational grazing

    The herd moves to a fresh paddock every three days from May through October. In winter they eat hay cut from our own fields, fed out on the hill.

  2. II

    Spring calving

    Calves arrive in April and May, outdoors. We record birth weight, calving ease, and mothering for every dam.

  3. III

    Handled from birth

    Calves are handled daily and halter-started before weaning, so they lead, load, and stand quietly for the vet.

  4. IV

    No grain

    Grass, hay, and free-choice minerals, nothing else. Animals leave in working condition, not show condition.
