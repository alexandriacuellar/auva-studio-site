Our story

Four generations on the same land

The farm began in 1921 with hill pasture, a few Highland heifers, and a house still to build. A hundred years on, the fields are the same, the herd is larger, and the family is still here.

1921 established4 generations52 head

Where Highland resilience meets an open gate

For more than a century, our family has worked this Hudson Valley land, keeping a ranching tradition passed down through four generations. At the heart of it is the breed: a hundred years spent refining the bloodlines, strength, and resilience of our Highland cattle.

Today we open our gates so you can see that living history for yourself. Watch traditional breeding up close, ride the trails our ancestors cut, and stay in our century-old family home. This land and this herd are our family’s greatest pride, and we would love to share a story written in the soil.

1921 to today

A hundred years, briefly

  1. 1921

    The land

    Our great-grandparents buy the hill farm in Ulster County and bring home their first Highland heifers.

  2. 1920s

    The farmhouse

    They build the farmhouse above the lower pasture, with a stone hearth and floors from timber cut on the hill.

  3. 1924

    The herd book

    The first registered Highland bull goes into the family herd book. The Glen Ard line starts here.

  4. 1962

    The Kinloch line

    Our grandparents bring in Kinloch cattle for hardiness and heavy winter coats, and cut the trails we still ride.

  5. 1987

    Blackwillow Angus

    Our parents start a small registered Angus herd, kept purebred alongside the Highlands.

  6. 1990

    The first crosses

    The first Highland × Angus calves are born on the farm.

  7. 1998

    The Ben Rua line

    A new Highland outcross, chosen for conformation. The dun and silver coats come with it.

  8. Today

    An open gate

    We keep 52 head, sell a few animals each year, and welcome riders, visitors, and overnight guests.

Since the 1920s

The farmhouse

The first generation built the farmhouse in the 1920s, on a rise above the lower pasture. Every generation since has lived in it. The floors, the stone hearth, and the windows are original. The kitchen and the plumbing, thankfully, are not.

Today the house is where our heritage stay guests sleep, where breakfast is served after a corral milking, and where the herd book is kept.

Heritage stays

What has not changed

  • The same fields

    The herd grazes the hill pastures our great-grandparents cleared, moved on every few days.

  • The same herd book

    Every calf born here since 1924 has a page. We still write the calving notes by hand.

  • Temperament first

    A quiet animal is easier to keep, easier to sell, and safer for children. We breed for it.

  • One of us at the gate

    Every visit, ride, and stay is led by a member of the family. No staff, no tour guides.
